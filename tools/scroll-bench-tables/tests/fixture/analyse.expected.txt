## 4. layout, the `full_paint` flag, and the frame that had nothing to draw

## 6. what a frame re-rastered, against what the scroll uncovered

`need` is the strip the frame's steps brought on screen - steps since the last frame, times the 120 CSS px
of a step, times the scale, clipped to the window - as a share of the window. `tile` is what WebRender said
it re-rastered into its picture cache (`wr-phases tiles .. kpx`, the dirty rect of every tile target, from
the patch in patches/webrender/0003). `ratio` is tile over need, so 1.0 is a renderer that draws exactly
what moved and 3.0 one that redraws two windows' worth of unchanged tiles for each one it needed.

`scroll` counts the frames a step moved; `idle` the ones whose step index did not advance. A run keeps
recording until 300 ms after the last frame, so a page that has reached the bottom, or that repaints itself
(yt's video, wiki's late loads), runs to its time budget with hundreds of idle frames. On the two static
pages an idle frame has nothing new to show, so whatever it re-rasters is the waste spec 05 asks about; on
yt it is the next video frame, so idle means something else there - and so, for that matter, does a
scroll frame, since the video repaints whether or not the page moved: the 12x in yt's unpaced row is the
picture moving, not a cache being thrown away.

`need` is a model and not a measurement: it assumes every answered step moves the page. That holds
to the end on the synthetic one (section 5's last bucket of it still re-rasters a quarter of a window),
but a real page can swallow a step into an inner scroller or a carousel, so wiki's 0.00 ratio is the
model overstating the need and not a renderer that draws nothing. Read the ratio on the long page; the
`idle` columns carry no assumption at all, since the step index did not move whatever the page did.
